## 7. Why it holds attention (reasons, not outcomes)
1. **An open question stays visible for 57 s.** The empty checkboxes (measured from 9.07 s to the end) keep a yes/no
   question unresolved. This is consistent with curiosity as a felt information gap. That is a *hypothesis*: the
   source was reached only as a record [S21]. Looked + measured.
2. **The cuts arrive with new content.** 10 of 11 body jump cuts bring a new screen. Within-scene cuts are often
   missed while viewers follow the content [S19], so a jump every ~5 s tightens the speech without reading as a
   break. Measured (times) + looked (content).
3. **Clear section changes at 7.1 and 9.07 s.** Inset → wide question shot → test layout. Perceived event boundaries
   organise attention and memory [S20]. Measured.
4. **The face is always on screen and never covered.** `face.share` 1.0, and all own text sits above the head
   (y 17.5) or below the chin (y 52–56). Faces draw early fixations [S2]. Measured.
5. **The promise is on screen from frame 0** [S9]. Measured.

## 9. Take this, don't copy that
| element | take (the system) | make your own (never copy) |
|---|---|---|
| structure | claim → question → source → steps → result → verdict | your own claims, your own test |
| question pill + question shot | a series question at the top, then one wide shot asking it | your own series name and wording; not "Fact or Hype" |
| checkbox bar | two options kept open until the result | your own two labels, colours and icons |
| inset of the claim | show the thing being judged at frame 0 | only material you may show; never re-post someone's clip whole. Prefer a screenshot or link card of a public page, or your own recording |
| test panel | your own screen recording, one clip per spoken step, cut on the sentence | your own recordings; never their footage |
| rhythm | about 11 body shot cuts/min; a jump cut per step | — |
| look | medium shot, desk, mic visible | your own set, palette and type; not teal frame + yellow italic together |
