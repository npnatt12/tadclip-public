# kamchai: split-screen Thai explainer (format `kamchai-split-explainer` v2, from 2 of 3 reels)

A fast Thai explainer about a product or tool, built on a split screen. Footage fills the top half and the seated
speaker (mic in shot, bookshelf behind) sits in the bottom half (`face.top` 45–56). The hook opens split, with a 2–3 row
heavy white headline that has one yellow phrase (`#F8E838`, y 46–47, `line_h` 6–7) and the product's name on the
footage. It ends at the first layout switch, 2.5 s in one reel and 7.7 s in the other. The body alternates blocks of
2.5–8.6 s. Split blocks carry one-line seam captions on a dark strip (y 49.5–50.3). Full-speaker blocks carry a big
chest punch phrase (y 60–65, keyword yellow) and sometimes a black-on-yellow label above the head (y 23–24). One reel
adds full-frame cutaways with a centred phrase. Measurer v2 says what drives the 36–46 detections a minute: **73–82 %
are shot cuts** (29.9–33.3/min; 30–38/min in the body), so the speaker's own shot is cut every ~1.4–1.6 s. About 30 % of
those are layout switches and the rest mostly jump cuts of the speaker. Insert changes (18–28 %) are partly B-roll
swaps inside the split (in `Detm2VPS1Mp`) and partly graphics appearing (all four in `DeUOm5syXua`). Version 1's even
three-way split overstated the B-roll swaps. The voice has no pauses, and one reel ends on a comment-keyword CTA with
the handle. `DeQr_7OKgcZ` (no face, a phone filming a monitor, posted from a different account handle) is excluded. The
deciding question for a new clip: is there footage for half the reel, and can the speech be cut at nearly every
sentence start without losing words?
