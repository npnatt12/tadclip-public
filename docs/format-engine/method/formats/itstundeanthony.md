# itstundeanthony: "Fact or Hype?" (format `itstundeanthony-fact-or-hype` v2, from 3 of 4 reels)

This is a test-the-claim series, carried by layout and a jump-cut test rather than by text. For the first 6.4–8.8 s
(the measured hook end) the claim under test plays in a rounded inset on the left: another creator's clip, or a repo
page with a picture-in-picture. Tags pop on it to state what makes the claim surprising (free, open source, a star
count). The speaker stands to the right (`face.cx` up to 62.8) under a white "Fact or Hype?" pill (y 17.3–17.5,
`line_h` 3.7–4.2). A hard cut to a wider full-frame shot lands "IS IT FACT OR HYPE" in huge yellow italic on the chest
(y 55–60, `line_h` 8.75). That shot is **1.97 s long in all three reels**. Then a FACT / OR / HYPE checkbox bar sits
across the chest (y 50–52) to the end. Under it a panel fills the lower half: first the claim's own page, unframed,
for 2.4–6 s, then a teal-framed screen recording of him running the test. Measurer v2 corrects the v1 reading on cuts.
After the hook, **90–93 % of detections are shot cuts**. The speaker's face jumps at almost every cut, and each jump
cut brings the next panel clip, so he is cut every 2.7–5 s (median), about 11–14 times a minute. Version 1 had
assumed the panel changed while he stood still. Two hook "shot cuts" artefacts come from the second face in the inset
and are corrected by hand. His speech has no captions, and the voice is tight (≤ 2.7 pauses/min). The fourth reel
(`C1-K1UfM_eB`, 360x640, typed code under language labels) shares no device and is excluded. The deciding question for
a new clip: does the user have a real test with screen recordings, cut into steps that each start on a new sentence?
