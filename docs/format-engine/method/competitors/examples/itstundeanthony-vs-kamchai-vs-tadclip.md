# Competitor analysis: TadClip's own reels vs itstundeanthony vs kamchai

> Performance data: 0 of 10 reels have views (`views: null` in both study files; TadClip's reels have no study
> file). **No performance data, so this comparison is descriptive.**
> Category caveat: these accounts are not direct competitors. itstundeanthony tests AI tools in English. kamchai
> explains tech and business tools in Thai. TadClip's reels were made for two clients: a relationship coach (ClubGig:
> `kg01k`, `kg03`) and a clinic/consultancy (clinic: `clinic-after`). The example shows the method; the "category" here
> is *Thai and English talking-head explainers*, and table stakes are read in that loose sense.

## 0. Summary for the owner
- **What everyone does:** own text on screen at 0.0 s, the face on screen ≥ 77 % of the time and never covered,
  tight voice (≤ 2.7 pauses/min), a second visual layer (panel, split or cutaways) inside the first 10 s, and one
  accent colour on key words. TadClip's reels already meet all of these.
- **Where TadClip stands:** a middle pace (15–20 shot cuts/min in the body, between itstundeanthony's 11–14 and
  kamchai's 30–38), the most own text of a brand (chrome, plated labels, end card), and the only reels with brand
  chrome on every frame and a logo end card.
- **Gaps to own:** (1) **proof the owner made**: TadClip's reels illustrate with stock only, while itstundeanthony
  shows his own screen; (2) **captions on the speech in full-speaker blocks**: nobody has them.
- **Resemblance to fix:** `kg01k` was built in kamchai's format and scores *close* to it (≈ 0.71). It shares his
  identifying look (white heavy Thai with one yellow phrase at a split seam, chest punch with a yellow keyword).
  Change the look and keep the system.
- **First three changes:** a ClubGig look for the split (wine plates instead of a yellow keyword); captions in
  full-speaker blocks; a real-proof device (with client permission) in place of some stock cutaways.

## 2. Per account
### itstundeanthony: "Fact or Hype?" (n = 3)
- **Format:** the claim in an inset beside the speaker, then a 1.97 s question shot, then a live test in a panel
  under two open checkboxes to the end. The body is jump-cut, and each cut starts the next test clip
  (`../method/formats/itstundeanthony.md`).
- **Hook pattern:** `claim-on-screen` + `question`. Hook end 6.37–8.80 s. Frame 0: the inset, the pill and the
  face; the promise is visible at 0.0 s.
- **Signature devices:** claim inset, claim tags, series pill, question shot, checkbox bar, source page, test panel.
- **Confidence:** n = 3; De1zlGniBQp's sheet stops at 59.0 of 63.9 s.

### kamchai: split-screen Thai explainer (n = 2)
- **Format:** footage on top and the speaker below at frame 0 under a promise headline, then alternating split
  blocks (seam captions) and full-speaker blocks (chest punch words, a yellow label). The speaker's shot is cut about
  every 1.5 s (`../method/formats/kamchai.md`).
- **Hook pattern:** `promise-headline`. Hook end 2.50–7.67 s. Frame 0: product footage, a headline with a yellow
  phrase, the face below; the promise is visible at 0.0 s.
- **Signature devices:** split, promise headline, product wordmark on footage, seam captions, chest punch, yellow
  label.
- **Confidence:** n = 2; Detm2VPS1Mp's sheet stops at 29.7 of 52.2 s.

## 7. What views and engagement would add
None of the 10 reels has view counts. With **public counts** for 3–5 reels per account, you could rank each
account's reels and see whether, inside one account, one hook pattern tends to do better (normalise by the account's
median; n is small, so treat it as anecdote). With the **owner's own insights**, you could link edits to outcomes for
your reels: Instagram's views, watch time and average watch time per initial view [S18]; on YouTube Shorts, the share
who viewed versus swiped away [S16] and the retention curve [S10]. That is how rec. 7 (and the pace in rec. 6)
should be decided. This data is optional; the analysis above stands without it, but it describes, it does not rank.
