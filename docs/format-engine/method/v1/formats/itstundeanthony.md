# itstundeanthony: "Fact or Hype?" (format `itstundeanthony-fact-or-hype`, from 3 of 4 reels)

This is a test-the-claim series, and its layout and structure carry it, not text density. For the first 6–9 s the
claim being tested plays in a rounded inset on the left: another creator's clip with its own captions, or a repo page
with a picture-in-picture. The speaker stands to its right (`face.cx` up to 62.8), under a white "Fact or Hype?" pill
(y 17.3–17.5, `line_h` 3.7–4.2, black on `#FEFEFE`). A cut to a wider full-frame shot then lands "IS IT FACT OR HYPE"
word by word in huge yellow italic with a black outline on the chest (y 55–60, `line_h` 8.75, `#F8E808`, 1.5–2 s).
After that, two things stay up to the end and cover 81–86 % of the reel: a FACT / OR / HYPE checkbox bar across the
chest (`text.chrome` y 50–52, an emoji after HYPE) and a teal-framed panel over the lower half that shows him running
the test. The panel changes content every few seconds. Those changes cause most of the 13–21 cuts/min, so his voice is
cut far less often than the number suggests. He is filmed in a medium shot (`face.height` ≈ 17–18 %, top 23–28 %), the
voice is tight (≤ 2.7 pauses/min), and his own speech has no captions. The fourth reel (C1-K1UfM_eB, 360x640, typed
code snippets under language labels) shares none of these devices and is excluded as an older series. The
fit-checker's main question for a new clip is whether it contains a real test with screen recordings. Without one,
only the pill and the stack transfer.
